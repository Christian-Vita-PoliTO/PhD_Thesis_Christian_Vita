\chapter{Conclusions and Perspectives}\label{ch:conclusions}

This thesis has worked on the data that stand between the Monte Carlo
reference and the many neutronic evaluations that the design and the operation
of a reactor require: the multi-group constants of a deterministic solver for
two lead-cooled fast reactors, and a database of fission matrices for a
heat-pipe micro reactor. In both cases the data were prepared once from a few
reference calculations, improved in the way they are built and corrected
without modifying the solver that uses them, and every result was set against a
Monte Carlo calculation made for that purpose. The conclusions of the two Parts
are gathered here, followed by what the two have in common and by the
developments that the work leaves open.

\section{Multi-group data for lead-cooled fast reactors}

For the Westinghouse LFR, the group constants were generated with Serpent~2
along two routes, from a single full-core model and from a set of mini-core
models representative of the regions of the system, and used in a
finite-element diffusion solver. The mini-core route pays off in accuracy and
in computational time whenever several configurations of the core have to be
analysed, since a full-core model has to be rerun for each of them, at a cost
that grows when low statistical errors are wanted in the non-multiplying
regions. The superhomogenization correction of the regions responsible for the
largest discrepancies brings the multiplication factor and the power density
distribution of the deterministic model close to the reference, at a cost of
seconds per supercell, and it does so more effectively where the system is more
heterogeneous. The kinetic parameters respond to the correction without a
direct benefit, which follows from what the correction preserves: reaction
rates, the product of a cross section and the flux, and not bilinear quantities
weighted with the adjoint. Fast reactors proved favourable ground for the
method, since the relatively uniform flux, the reduced anisotropy and the
smooth energy dependence of the cross sections limit the condensation errors
and let scalar factors preserve the global quantities.

For the ALFRED core, the energy grid on which the constants are condensed was
treated as the unknown of an optimization, and the dependence of the result on
the random seed, observed in the work this chapter built on, was measured on
one thousand independent searches instead of three. The number was made
affordable by a neural metamodel trained on $91\,723$ full-core evaluations of
FRENETIC, which replaced the solver inside the search alone; every structure
the searches returned was recomputed with the solver, so that the conclusions
rest on it and not on the metamodel. The dependence is severe: a single search
returns a structure typically forty-five per cent above the best on the joint
objective, three searches twenty per cent, and a hundred two per cent, so that
the gain from repeating the search lies precisely in the range that the
reference solver cannot reach. The thousand searches converged to 747 distinct
structures that share no common skeleton, and what they do share is a single
exclusive choice in the fast region and a placement of the boundaries that
follows the neutron importance rather than the flux, a criterion never imposed
and found a posteriori. The best structure has fifteen groups and improves on
the three obtained with the solver inside the loop, on the structures built by
uniform spacing rules and on the finest structure the collapsing procedure
admits, the last by five orders of magnitude on the joint objective. That last
comparison is the one that explains the result: a structure that condenses
almost nothing leaves the error of the deterministic model intact, and the
optimized structure does better because its condensation error cancels that
model error. The quality of the best structures is therefore bound to the core,
to the solver and to the objective on which it was obtained, and what carries
over to another case is the procedure, whose cost was repaid after nine
searches and which delivered analyses worth of the order of $390\,000$
core-hours of the reference solver for twenty-nine.

\section{Fission matrix methods for heat-pipe micro reactors}

For the heat-pipe micro reactor of the Virtual Test Bed, a database of 136
fission matrices over eight fuel temperatures and seventeen drum angles, with a
density settled by a convergence study and a prescribed correlation between
power and fuel temperature, supports a criticality search that closes on the
fission matrix alone. At nominal power and fresh fuel the search converges in
about one second on a single core, uncertainty quantification included, and the
multiplication factor at the state it returns deviates from a reference Monte
Carlo calculation by $-56\pm16$~pcm, which is the residual of the interpolation
of the database. The extension to burnup adds no axis to the database: burnup
enters the interpolated matrix as a multiplicative correction ratio tallied
once per snapshot time, and a quasi-static cycle in which drum position,
temperature field, fission source and cell-wise burnup are updated together
keeps the multiplication factor of the states it returns within $-59$ to $-5$
pcm of unity over sixty-six months, with the bias already present at beginning
of life and no trend with burnup, and the assembly-wise fission source within
about one per cent. The three assumptions of the factorization were measured
on dedicated campaigns and found at or below that residual, and seven snapshot
times suffice on the burnup axis.

Two effects that an isothermal database cannot represent were corrected with
ratios of the same kind, each with its own reference case and its own weight.
The reflector correction restores the temperature difference between core and
reflector and reduces the deviation of the multiplication factor from several
hundred pcm to a few tens on a uniformly hot core, and below the statistical
uncertainty of the reference once the reference matrix follows the rotation of
the drums; on non-uniform fields it recovers almost the whole discrepancy when
the field is smooth and only part of it when the profile is steep from ring to
ring. The core temperature correction restores the influence of the
neighbouring cells on each coefficient and, over twelve temperature fields,
reduces the mean absolute deviation of the multiplication factor by a quarter
and that of the fission source by as much, with a benefit concentrated on the
fields that are sharp and extended over several cells. The statistical
uncertainty of the interpolated results was examined in full: the coefficients
that share a source column are negatively correlated because a fixed number of
histories is exhausted among mutually exclusive destinations, the correlation
is predicted in closed form from the mean coefficients and the multiplicity
alone, and against a brute-force reconstruction from 150 independent databases
the closed form reproduces the uncertainty of the multiplication factor to
within five per cent, while the uncorrelated assumption used elsewhere in the
Part overestimates it, so that the bands reported for the criticality search
are conservative.

\section{What the two Parts share}

The two Parts differ in the reactor, in the solver and in the data, and they
agree on how the data were treated. The corrections developed in both,
superhomogenization factors on one side and correction ratios on the other,
are computed from a small number of reference calculations and applied to the
data, so that the solver receives a corrected input and needs no modification:
the approaches are non-intrusive, which is what makes them applicable to a
code that cannot be altered. In both Parts the reference kept the last word,
and where a further layer of approximation was introduced for speed, the neural
metamodel of the energy-grid search, it was allowed to explore while the solver
decided. In both the reference was treated as the statistical estimate it is,
in the thresholds below which two structures are not told apart and in the
uncertainty that the interpolated matrices inherit from the tallies.

The two Parts also agree on the limits of what they obtained, and the limit is
of the same nature in both. A correction calibrated on reference cases holds at
the conditions of those cases: the superhomogenization factors are bound to the
configuration on which they were computed, the optimized energy grid to the
core, the solver and the objective whose errors it makes cancel, the
correction ratios to the reference states and fields on which they were
calibrated. None of this weakens the results, but it says what carries over to
another core or another solver, and that is the procedure rather than the
numbers it produced here.

\section{Perspectives}

Each of the works leaves a direction open, and they are stated as the chapters
stated them. On the multi-group side, the mini-core route to the generation of
the constants lends itself to uncertainty and sensitivity analyses related to
nuclear data in transient calculations, and the reproduction of the kinetic
parameters under superhomogenization correction, which preserves reaction rates
and not adjoint-weighted quantities, remains to be improved. The accuracy of
the neural metamodel is still limited by the amount of reference data, and the
data that would help most are those in the region where the target is
steepest, around the optimized structures and their one-boundary neighbours,
which a targeted campaign of the reference solver could supply; the description
of a structure given to the network could also be enriched with
physics-informed quantities, such as the lethargy widths of the condensed
groups or the variation of the reference spectrum within them weighted by the
importance, a possibility that a preliminary attempt did not exhaust.

On the fission matrix side, the residual of the criticality search is the
interpolation of the original database, and the prescribed correlation between
power and fuel temperature can be replaced by a thermal response from a
conduction solver, which the interface of the workflow already allows. The two
temperature corrections were developed on different models and applied
separately; their combined application requires the calibration of both on the
same model, and a weight that does not count twice a field that is hot overall
and sharp inside, in the outermost ring of assemblies where the two effects
overlap. Where the temperature profile is steep from ring to ring, a zonation
of the reference matrix would address the part of the discrepancy that the
reflector correction leaves.
